Top picks

1. Outbreaks are seasonal, not spread evenly across the year (finding #6, #10)
- H₀: Each disease's outbreak records are evenly distributed across the 12
  months.
- H₁: They are concentrated in particular months.
- Test: Chi-square goodness-of-fit on monthly record counts, run once per
  disease. This should come out clearly significant for ADD, Dengue, Cholera
  and Malaria. AES is a good "maybe not" case because it only has 111 records.

2. Vector-borne diseases respond to rainfall with a delay; waterborne disease
responds immediately (finding #11)
- H₀: For Dengue, the correlation between outbreak count and rainfall one
  month earlier (lag 1) is no stronger than with same-month rainfall (lag 0).
- H₁: The lag-1 correlation is stronger.
- Test: Steiger's test for two correlations that share a variable. Also test
  whether each correlation differs from zero.
- Caveat: monthly series are autocorrelated, so the standard p-values will
  look better than they should. A permutation or block-bootstrap p-value is
  the defensible version, and it would earn you marks for rigour.

how many hypothesis tests should i run ?

Response 32 lines 
● Run 3 to 4 main hypotheses. Your own spec.md already plans for 2–4 ("Finalize
2–4 concrete, testable hypotheses before moving from EDA to modeling"), so
this fits.

Why that number:
- Fewer than 3 looks thin for a project whose goal is to "identify and test
  hypotheses".
- More than 5 tends to become a list of p-values with no story. It also raises
  the multiple-comparisons problem: at α = 0.05, roughly 1 in 20 tests will
  come out "significant" by pure chance.
- 3–4 is enough to use several different tests (chi-square, correlation,
  Kruskal-Wallis) and still discuss each one properly: assumptions, result,
  effect size and interpretation.

Hypotheses vs. individual tests: One hypothesis can involve several tests. For
example, #1 (seasonality) runs once per disease, which is about 8 tests, and
#3 has post-hoc pairwise comparisons. That's fine, but apply a Bonferroni or
Holm correction within each family, and mention it in the writeup. Graders
usually look for this.

Suggested set:
1. Seasonality (chi-square goodness-of-fit, per disease)
2. Rainfall lag, lag 1 vs. lag 0 (correlation / Steiger's test)
3. Climate conditions by disease (Kruskal-Wallis + Dunn post-hoc)
4. (Optional) Outbreak size by disease (Kruskal-Wallis)

If you want a fifth, choose the LAI one (#8). It will probably fail to reject
H₀, and including one honest negative result makes the analysis look unbiased.

If your course rubric gives a specific number, go with that instead.

● Here's what each test can answer with your data. Since Steiger's test isn't on
your list, H1 needs to change to fit these tests (see the bottom).

Comparing groups (numeric value vs. categories)

t-test (2 groups, assumes roughly normal data)
- Is mean temperature at outbreak time different for Dengue vs. Malaria?
- Is mean monthly rainfall higher in monsoon months than in non-monsoon
  months?
- ⚠️ Don't use it on Cases. It's far too skewed. Temperature is the variable
  closest to normal.

Mann-Whitney U (2 groups, the non-parametric version of the t-test)
- Do Dengue outbreaks have larger case counts than Chikungunya outbreaks?
- Do Chikungunya outbreaks occur farther south (lower latitude) than Dengue
  outbreaks?
- Are monthly outbreak counts higher in monsoon months (Jun–Sep) than in other
  months?
- Did outbreaks in 2020 have fewer cases than in 2010–2019 (the COVID effect)?

ANOVA (3+ groups, assumes normality and equal variances)
- Does mean temperature at outbreak time differ across diseases?
- Does mean temperature differ across regions (N/S/E/W/NE)?
- Check the assumptions first: Shapiro-Wilk for normality and Levene for equal
  variances.

Kruskal-Wallis (3+ groups, the non-parametric version of ANOVA)
- Does typical outbreak size (Cases) differ across the 8 diseases? This is the
  right test for the box plot finding.
- Does rainfall at outbreak time differ across diseases?
- Does outbreak size differ across regions?

Categories vs. categories

Chi-square
- Goodness-of-fit: Are each disease's outbreaks spread evenly across the 12
  months? (H2)
- Independence: Is disease type independent of region? (H4)
- Independence: Is disease type independent of season (pre-monsoon / monsoon /
  post-monsoon / winter)?
- Independence: Is the share of outbreaks that recorded at least one death the
  same across diseases?
  - ⚠️ Weak evidence: 71.6% of Deaths was missing and filled with 0.

Relationships between two numeric variables

Pearson (linear relationship, sensitive to outliers)
- Rainfall at month t−1 vs. Dengue outbreak count at month t (H1).
- Mean monthly temperature vs. mean monthly LAI.

Spearman (monotonic relationship based on ranks, robust to outliers)
- Year vs. annual ADD outbreak count: is it declining?
- Rainfall vs. Cases at the record level. The skew means Spearman fits better
  than Pearson here.
- Lag correlations again, this time robust to extreme months.
